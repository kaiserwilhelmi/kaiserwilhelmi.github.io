Forecasting 30,490 Walmart products at once

The M5 competition asked for 28 days of daily unit sales for every item in 10 Walmart stores. 5,507 teams entered in 2020. I worked through the same problem in 2026 under two rules: only the competition data, and only methods published before the competition ended. Then I submitted my forecasts to Kaggle to get the official score.

Kaggle scored the model 0.55981 on the hidden test period. That beats the team that finished 50th, so it would sit in the top 1% of the final standings. Late entries get no official rank.

How I built it

  1. Set the test before touching models. Four 28-day practice windows from the past, and a rule fixed in advance: a change stays only if it helps on average and wins at least 3 of the 4 windows. The real test period was scored once, at the end.
  2. Beat the simple methods first. Croston-style intermittent demand methods times each item's own weekday pattern scored 0.69. Every model after that had to earn its keep against 0.69, not against the easy 0.89 benchmark.
  3. Gradient-boosted trees, one model per store and forecast week. LightGBM with a Poisson loss, fed recent sales, calendar and events, SNAP days, prices and out-of-stock streaks. Forecasting each week ahead with its own model beat both one big model and day-by-day chaining.
  4. Make the levels agree. Item forecasts added up disagree with forecasts of store and department totals, so a second model forecasts the totals and the two are reconciled. That one step improved the score more than any feature.
  5. Verify everything twice. The scorer, the features and the final numbers each have an independent second implementation. My own holdout score matched Kaggle's public score to four decimal places: 0.5897 against 0.58972.

The full write-up, with the validation design, every experiment and the references, is in the project report (PDF).

Data

Walmart daily sales for 3,049 products in 10 stores across California, Texas and Wisconsin, 2011 to 2016, with prices, calendar events and SNAP days. Published for the M5 competition by the University of Nicosia's MOFC and hosted on Kaggle. Top-five scores from Makridakis, Spiliotis & Assimakopoulos (2022), International Journal of Forecasting.
