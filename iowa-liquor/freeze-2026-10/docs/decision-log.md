# Iowa liquor supplier demand forecast: decision log

Owner: design thread ("Build Iowa liquor supplier demand forecast"). Started Nov 27 2026.
Plan doc: https://claude.ai/artifact/K5SMhUy72BEPgsH2vin5GV (its "Current plan" section on top overrides older sections).
Purpose: every design decision, why it was made, the evidence, and what is NOT verified, so independent reviewers can challenge each one.
Status key: D = decided by Dawson, R = ruled by the design thread (open to challenge), U = unverified claim.

## Source and data
1. (R) Source of truth = Iowa Department of Revenue, Iowa Data Hub (data.iowa.gov), per-year datasets; full history dataset 1051 has 35,287,731 rows, Jan 2 2012 to Oct 29 2026, CC BY, monthly updates (last Nov 5 2026). Verified on the publisher's pages Nov 27.
2. (R) Pull method = undocumented address /api/dataset-download?path=datasets/<id>/rows.csv (zip of CSV parts). Works without cookies from a browser. (U) not tested from a non-browser client or GitHub Actions; site is behind Cloudflare.
3. (R) Four yearly zips (2020, 2021, 2022, 2025) contain a duplicate part_0005; dropped; total then matches 35,287,731 (coding thread, to be re-verified).
4. (R) Some rows have decimals truncated to whole numbers in state_bottle_cost, state_bottle_retail, sales_dollars, sales_liters, sales_gallons (seen on INV-903479, 2026). Volume = sales_bottles x bottle_volume_ml / 1000; dollars recomputed as bottles x the item's untruncated price (modal price per item per month). (U) extent by year unknown.
5. (R) A row = one line of a store's order from the state warehouse. "Sales" = sales by the state to stores, not shelf sales. sales_dollars uses state_bottle_retail (price the store pays). (U) state retail appears to be cost x 1.5 (two sample rows).
6. (U) BigQuery public copy (bigquery-public-data.iowa_liquor_sales) currency not verified; not used as source.
7. (U) Iowa bailment warehouse (vendor-owned stock) recalled from memory, not verified.

## Framing
8. (D) Main question: how much demand (volume and sales dollars) should be expected from the state of Iowa each month for each brand and product; top liquors featured. Supplier/vendor is a model input and roll-up, not the main level.
9. (R) Statewide store orders summed = "depletions", which is what a supplier's demand planner forecasts. Not consumer demand.
10. (D) Nothing on the page is simulated. No simulated stock, fill rate or savings.
11. (D) Later views: warehouse reorder points (recommendations for stated lead times, checked by real exceedance), store x category monthly ("what this store will order"). (R) These may land after the release date.
12. (D) Phase 2 candidates, not approved: market shifts/premiumization, assortment long tail + launch survival, supplier concentration. (R) Elasticity and market basket judged weak/risky on this data.

## Modeling
13. (R) Primary grain: product x calendar month; zero months filled between first and last order. Hierarchy: item -> brand -> category -> state.
14. (R) Brand derived by rule from im_desc for all items; top 50 by trailing-12-month dollars hand-checked; rule-derived brands flagged.
15. (R) Each product assigned to its current vendor (latest order), because brands move between vendors.
16. (R) Units: 9-liter cases (liters/9), bottles, dollars.
17. (R) Calendar: order-day count per month as a model input; check whether the latest month is complete (data ends Oct 29 2026).
18. (D, method by R) Ensemble: per-series statistical models (seasonal exponential smoothing; Croston, SBA, TSB, ADIDA for intermittent series) + one global LightGBM with quantile loss across all products; quantile averaging with equal weights first, learned weights per demand class only if they beat equal weights; conformal calibration of ranges; point forecasts reconciled across the hierarchy (MinT), ranges per level not forced coherent.
19. (R) Each layer must beat the simpler layer below it on validation or it is dropped; seasonal naive and the single best simple method stay on the page as baselines.
20. (R) Demand classes by Syntetos-Boylan-Croston cutoffs (ADI 1.32, CV^2 0.49), used to organise results, class window 2024-2025.
21. (R) Selection metric: quantile (pinball) loss and coverage. Selection on rolling monthly origins 2023-2025; frozen test Jan-Oct 2026 run once.
22. (D) Compute must stay reasonable: one global model, no deep learning, about seven quantiles, no monthly retraining.
23. (R) Freeze forecasts for Nov 2026 onward with a timestamp before the state posts November data (assumed about Dec 5 2026, inferred from the Nov 5 update). Monthly scorecard grades frozen forecasts against new data.

## Known open questions for reviewers
- Is rolling-origin selection over 2023-2025 plus a 10-month frozen test enough to choose among ensembles without a winner's curse? (Forecast Compare audit found selection lost to a fixed combination.)
- Should 2020-2021 (pandemic) be down-weighted or flagged in training?
- Forecasting dollars directly versus volume x price path.
- How to treat new products, discontinued products, and brand renames in the scorecard.
- Is a forecast made in late November for November (data through October) fairly called out-of-sample? Horizon labelling.
- Academic citations in the plan doc are from memory and not yet checked against publisher pages.

32. (R) Dollars. Before Jul 2025: use the state's sales_dollars as given (no unit price exists). Reason: a truncated line total loses under $1 per line, a truncated unit price loses up to $1 per bottle. Report by year: dollars by source, and the upper bound of the loss (number of raw-total lines x $1) as a share of dollars. Supersedes the same-item-month-only rule in 25.
33. (R) No "dollars at state cost", supplier revenue or margin view in the first release: cost exists for about 16 months only. One dollar measure: store purchases at the state's price. State-price inputs to the model use implied price = sales_dollars / bottles (item-month median), which exists in every year. (U) whether pre-2025 sales_dollars are also truncated: check the share of whole-number line totals by year against the rate on clean 2025-26 rows.
34. (R) No line-level key exists. Keep every row after the duplicate-part drop (yearly counts match the publisher). Report exact full-row duplicates by year as a caveat; do not remove them. The publisher's "unique identifier: invoice_id" label is wrong for these files; say so in the data notes.
35. (R) store_no normalised by stripping leading zeros; report how many stores merge. Affects the later store view only.
36. (R) Order days (replaces the test in 29). Weekend lines are about 1.6% of all lines, so the count of distinct order dates overstates working days. Test the weekday-less-holidays calendar against "full" days (days with bottles at or above 25% of that month's median day; the 25% is the coding thread's choice) and report the share of bottles on non-calendar days by year. Pass = the judge's rule (within one day in 95% of complete months, no consistent drift) on full days AND under 2% of bottles on non-calendar days in every year. If it fails in any era: effective order days = sum over weekdays of (count of that weekday in the month, holidays removed) x (that weekday's share of volume over the trailing 24 months as of the origin, scaled so a standard week = 5). Weights are known at the origin and the weekday counts come from the calendar, so nothing looks ahead.
37. (R) Published date range on the dataset page (Jan 2 2012 to Oct 29 2026) is one day earlier than the data (Jan 3 2012 to Oct 30 2026); treated as a display offset on the state's page. Use the data.

## Unit breaks and mini packs (Nov 27 2026, ~7:15 PM PT; rulings pending judge)
Evidence from the coding thread: (i) 166 steps on 138 items where dollars per bottle jumps 1.8x or more while dollars stay smooth; 51 items have trailing-12 sales ($5.07M, about 1.2%). Example: every Sazerac 99 mini went from about $7.74 to $77.40 per unit in May-Jun 2023 with units down 8 to 10x; Tito's 38449 from $15.73 to $126 in Dec 2025. So the state changed what one "bottle" is, inside the fit window. (ii) 205 smooth whole-multiple size changes, mostly minis recorded at 300/500/600 ml until Aug 2019 and 50 ml after. (iii) 119 series ($22.5M trailing-12) have dollars per liter 4x or more their brand's median with no size history, mostly mini sleeves and buckets; pack counts cannot be proven from the data. 15621 and 84172 confirmed 0.6 L per unit in Jul 2019.
50. (R) Unit breaks are restated for modelling. A break = dollars per bottle steps 1.8x or more between the 3 months before and after (medians), while monthly dollars stay smooth. Pre-break bottles are divided by the price ratio rounded to a whole number, so the whole history is in the unit the state uses now, which is the unit frozen and graded. If the ratio is not within 10% of a whole number, no restatement: the series starts at the break and is treated as a new product. Rows in the transition months are assigned to the old or new unit by their own dollars per bottle. Threshold fixed now, before any score. Hand check of the top 30 breaks by trailing-12 dollars, pass at 2 wrong or fewer. Dollars are never changed. Classes and order-day shares are rebuilt after restatement. Live rule: a break of the same kind after the freeze is restated into frozen units for grading and shown beside the grade.
51. (R) Liters are a secondary measure, in three evidence tiers per item and period in the units table. Proven: the state's own size history gives the factor (larger / smaller recorded size on rows at the smaller size, chained with any unit break from 50); genuine size changes found by hand are excluded (Foundry Gin 375 to 750, Cedar Ridge barrel, Bruichladdich 4500).
