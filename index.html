Purchasing and supply chain tools

Tools I built while studying supply chain at CSU San Marcos and working as a controller. Most of them use public government data. When a project uses sample data, the page says so.

Industry Market Watch

17 industries · 80 cost series · updated daily

Each of the 17 industries has a page that tracks the costs behind its prices, like materials, wages, fuel, freight and exchange rates. Each cost gets a 6-month forecast, a rating of Alert, Watch or Opportunity, and a short note on what a buyer could do about it.

Open Market Watch
Data
BLS, EIA, IMF, Federal Reserve, Cass, USDA, Census and USAspending

Supplier Scorecard

Sample data, or upload your own purchase orders

I made up 12 office-supply suppliers and about 2,000 purchase orders, then scored each supplier on on-time delivery, fill rate, defects, invoice accuracy and price compliance. The chart shows where a lot of money goes to a supplier that scores low.

Forecast Method Comparison

Up to 14 methods · any of the 100 Market Watch series, or your own file

The tool tests each method by having it forecast past months it hasn't seen, then ranks the methods by how far off they were. The best method changes from one series to the next. I checked the results against a separate Python version.

Side project

Loot Ledger

Search any of the 5,111 items in the online game Tibia and it lists every NPC that buys it, highest price first, with a map to each one. Paste your loot after a hunt and it tells you who to sell each item to.

Item and map data come from TibiaWiki (CC BY-SA 3.0) and TibiaMaps.io.
